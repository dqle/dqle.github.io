About

Infrastructure that's boring by design

For the past ten years I've been replacing hand-built environments with systems that can be rebuilt on demand. That has meant greenfield GCP platforms built on my own Terraform modules, CI/CD pipelines that roll microservices out to GKE across multiple regions, and observability that keeps incidents short and post-mortems honest.

The work I'm proudest of is the work nobody sees: a deployment that goes through without drama, a certificate that renews itself, an alert that never needs to fire.

How I work

Three principles behind every platform decision

I hold each project I take on to these three standards.

01

Eliminate repetitive work

Anything I do more than once gets turned into a module, playbook or pipeline, and the payoff keeps growing from there. Examples: zero-downtime patching that rolls back on its own, hosts added to Nagios as part of the VM build, SSL renewals that need no human input, and GitOps deployments in place of manual console work.

TerraformAnsibleArgoCDHelm

02

Treat cost as part of the design

I plan cloud spend into the architecture from day one rather than finding it on the invoice. I traced orphaned disks and stale snapshots back to misconfigured backup and database deployments and removed more than $300,000 a year in wasted spend. I sized client environments with a tier-by-tier cost analysis before building them, and I replaced a paid DigiCert contract with free Google Trust Services certificates.

Cost analysisRight-sizingGCPAWS

03

Document so anyone can follow along

Self-service only works if people can actually serve themselves. My runbooks are written so a brand-new teammate can work through them alone at 3 AM. Each one has the exact commands, the output to expect, a way to roll back, and the reasoning behind every step. Good documentation means on-call no longer depends on one person.

RunbooksPost-mortemsOnboarding
